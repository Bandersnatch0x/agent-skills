# Review Lenses

Use the core lenses for every audit. Sample additional lenses according to actual risks; they are discovery prompts, not a mandatory expansion of scope.

## Six core lenses

### 1. Positioning and goal coherence

- What recurring or consequential problem warrants this product rather than the current workaround?
- Is the goal a user outcome, a business outcome, or merely a list of implementation outputs?
- Can the primary promise be demonstrated in one coherent task? Which capabilities do not contribute to it?
- Does ambition fit the available people, budget, integration access, and maintenance capacity?
- Which evidence would make narrowing, repositioning, or stopping more rational than adding features?

Output: provisional positioning, strongest contradiction, and a falsifiable assumption. Repository polish is not market validation.

### 2. Users and capability domains

- Who experiences the problem, performs the task, pays, maintains the system, and approves consequential decisions?
- What can each actor understand, access, afford, and authorize? What happens without a specialist's assistance?
- Is there one primary user/job for this cycle, or an accidental union of incompatible audiences?
- Which capabilities belong to this product, an upstream provider, a downstream consumer, or a human?
- What does each boundary accept, guarantee, reject, and hand off? Who owns the definitive decision or record?

Output: actor/job map and own/integrate/defer/exclude boundaries. Merge roles only when evidence supports doing so.

### 3. Workspace, builder workflow, and main path

- Do exports, routes, commands, build outputs, and deployment configuration match the stated product?
- Are decisions, intake, verification, release state, and next-work selection understandable without reconstructing history?
- Are generated files mistaken for sources, prototypes for production, fixtures for adoption, or old plans for current commitments?
- Does work have an owner and an exit at each transition, including rejection, interruption, and release failure?
- Are internal tools advertised as customer features, or customer entrypoints buried behind maintainer instructions?

Output: separate customer and builder maps, with the broken edge identified. Missing internal process alone does not justify a customer console.

### 4. Reachability, boundaries, and post-delivery closure

- Can the intended user discover, invoke, authorize, and complete the capability in the distributed product?
- Is a missing entrypoint required, or is the capability deliberately internal, experimental, or gated?
- Does the next actor receive a usable result, its limitations, and enough context to act without the original creator?
- Can an error, rejected result, changed source, or stale dependency return to the right owner without starting over?
- For recurring jobs, does feedback reach the next decision? For one-time jobs, is completion and exit explicit?

Output: unreachable value, ambiguous ownership, or broken handoff with a smallest repair. More screens are only one possible repair.

### 5. Friction and support burden

- Where do users wait, repeat information, translate identifiers, hunt for files, or need maintainer intervention?
- Which prerequisites appear too late? Which defaults are unsafe, misleading, or wrong for the target user?
- Do errors identify a cause, responsible actor, recovery action, and retained work?
- Are approvals tied to authority/risk, or duplicate rituals? Does removing one conceal a required decision?
- What is known about frequency, severity, recovery cost, and support cost? What remains unmeasured?

Output: friction at a specific transition, root cause, observable cost, and confidence. Do not invent prevalence or remove safety controls just to reduce clicks.

### 6. Next action and signature outcome

- On completion, failure, suspension, or rejection, does the next actor know what to do and when to stop?
- What smallest result makes the product worth choosing over the current alternative?
- Can existing capabilities connect into that result without broadening scope?
- What narrow scenario demonstrates the benefit, and what observation would disprove it?
- Does the next bet solve the primary job, or compensate for weak positioning with novelty?

Output: one next action and, when supportable, one signature-outcome hypothesis with a minimal proof. "Nothing new yet" is valid.

## Additional lenses for targeted brainstorming

| Lens | Question that changes a decision | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alternatives and switching | Why leave the current tool or manual workaround, and what migration cost could erase the benefit? | Positioning, adoption, replacement products |
| Distribution and first value | Can the right person find, install/access, and get a useful result before maintainer intervention? | First-run failure or confusing packaging |
| Economics and sustainability | Who pays in money, attention, compute, support, or maintenance; does the intended model cover that cost? | Commercial or resource-constrained products; do not force monetization on internal tools |
| Trust and authority | Can claims be traced, contested, corrected, and revoked by the right actor? | Automation, approvals, evidence, sensitive operations |
| Data and reversibility | Who owns inputs/outputs; can users export, migrate, roll back, or leave without hidden loss? | Persistent data, lock-in, consequential state |
| Operational lifecycle | What happens on partial failure, upgrade, dependency drift, unavailable providers, and retirement? | Integrations, installed software, long-lived services |
| Accessibility and exclusion | Which abilities, languages, devices, permissions, or network conditions make the primary job unreachable? | Affected audiences or constrained environments |
| Outcome measurement | What would show first value, repeated value, or false success without manufacturing evidence or unapproved telemetry? | Unvalidated claims, learning loops |
| Incentives and coordination | Does an operator's local success impose work or risk on another actor? | Teams, approvers, handoffs, marketplaces |
| Dependency sequence | Is a feature blocked by a real prerequisite, or a speculative platform built before a use case? | Stalled projects, oversized roadmaps |
| Portfolio subtraction | Which feature, workflow step, compatibility path, or public promise can be removed without losing the core outcome? | Feature accumulation or unsustainable ownership |
| Abandonment and terminal states | Can users cancel, decline, pause, recover, or finish cleanly rather than being forced into perpetual engagement? | Long tasks, retries, handoffs, one-shot products |

## Converge after brainstorming

For the highest-value gap, compare a connection of existing capability, a simplification/removal, a no-build experiment, and new capability only if needed. State the weakest assumption and the smallest observation that changes the recommendation. Keep rejected/deferred ideas visible without turning them into commitments.
